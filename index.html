Asking for an inspection visit before any cleaning is agreed

A request that asks for a look first separates two decisions: what the ducts and equipment are like, and what, if anything, should be done about it. Write it in that order. Start with the parts of your Etobicoke home a visit should see, since a visit may show what cannot be judged from photos, but only for parts the request names. Then note what it should record and what stays closed. Finally, keep the visit and any later work as separate steps, each with its own written scope.

  1. Return grilles

    Where room air is drawn back toward the furnace.

  2. Return ducts

    Carry that air back to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    The blower, evaporator coil and cabinet.

  4. Supply trunk

    The largest duct carrying air away from the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    Smaller ducts feeding each room.

  6. Supply registers

    Where air comes back into each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • Parts you want seen

    List the registers, return grilles, furnace or air handler, HRV and dryer vent that the visit should look at, room by room.

  • What the visit should produce

    Say whether you expect photos, written notes or both, and ask that anything the provider could not reach is listed.

  • Visit separate from cleaning

    Ask the provider to state the charge for looking on its own and to agree cleaning only after the findings are read.

Questions About Your Etobicoke Request

When I ask Etobicoke Duct Cleaning for an inspection visit, who am I actually dealing with?

You are dealing with an enquiry website, not a contractor. It has no staff to send out and carries out no visits or cleaning itself. Our enquiry team reads each Etobicoke request and may forward it to a responding provider. That provider must identify itself and confirm it can attend your address before anything is arranged.

What happens when I send a request for an inspection visit in Etobicoke?

Sending a request starts a review and nothing more: our enquiry team reads what you wrote. It does not book a visit, set a price or confirm that anyone covers your address. Any provider who responds then has to agree the visit, its written scope, its charge and its timing with you directly, before anything happens.

Should I ask for a visit before agreeing to duct cleaning at my Etobicoke home?

It is a choice, not a requirement. A visit first suits an owner who wants findings before deciding, while others may prefer a written quote on a clearly described job. Either way, say in the request which you want, so the responding provider knows whether to propose a visit, a cleaning or both as separate steps.

What should the written findings from a visit say about parts not reached?

Ask that the findings name each section, piece of equipment and vent that was viewed, how it was recorded in photographs or notes, and which parts could not be reached and why. A report that lists only what looked fine, or says nothing about limits, gives you little to compare against a later written quote. Ask for it before you reply.

Can the charge for a visit be kept separate from later cleaning work?

Yes, you can ask for it. State in the request that you want the visit priced as its own item, and that no cleaning, sealing or treatment is to be agreed until you have read the findings. The responding provider must then set out each step, its scope and its price in writing, so you can accept one without the others.

Will a visit confirm that my ducts have a problem or need cleaning?

Not necessarily. A visit records what the provider can see and reach, and a single register cannot stand in for every branch. The findings may support cleaning, suggest a different step or show that nothing needs doing, and no health benefit is promised in any case. Treat the outcome as information for your own decision. Keep it with your request.

Does an inspection visit cover my dryer vent as well as the ducts?

Only if the request says so. Name the dryer vent, the hood outside and any HRV or ERV you want included, and ask the provider to confirm each one in writing. Anything left out should be listed as not viewed rather than assumed, so the report shows what the visit did and did not cover.

Who has to agree before a visit to a rented or shared Etobicoke space?

Whoever controls the equipment and the building may need to agree, and that may be an owner, a landlord, a property manager or a condominium corporation. Ask them, not this site, what applies to your space. Name that person in the request, and ask the provider to confirm in writing who agreed to the visit, who pays for it and who will give access.
